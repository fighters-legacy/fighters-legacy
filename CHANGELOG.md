## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **test**: Honest-sensing acceptance scenarios (#693) — the cross-cutting tests that answer *"is the AI actually honest now?"* rather than *"does this function return the right number?"*. New `tests/test_honest_sensing.cpp` drives a real `WorldBroadcaster` tick with a real sensor-def resolver, and pins all four #670 acceptance bullets: **a bandit directly astern of a forward-looking AI is invisible to it**; **a contact is detected before it is reacted to** (seeing is not noticing); **a Cadet's radar reaches half as far as an Ace's** (a target placed *between* the two ranges is found by one and not the other); **a visual-only unit acquires more slowly at night than at noon**; and **contact tables are byte-identical on 1 worker and on 4** — the property replay (#644) and every scale-gate number quietly rest on. A sixth test states the whole thing in one place: move a target while it is outside the cone and the AI **keeps believing the old position** — its picture of the world is now wrong, honestly so, and there is no path from a contact table back to the truth. Added to the **TSan** job: the sensing pass's serial-equivalence guarantee is only worth as much as the absence of a race underneath it
- **docs**: Sensor, signature and detection authoring guide (#694) — the community-facing half of the sensor core. `docs/modding/formats.md` gains an end-to-end **"putting it together"** walkthrough (the sensor def, the aircraft that carries it and declares its own signatures, and the third file that does not exist — because omitting `sensors` still gets you the builtin eyeball: *honest sensing is the default, you cannot opt out of it, only tune it*). `docs/modding/ai.md` gains a **"what your script can and cannot see"** overview up front, where a script author hits it before writing a line — including the plain statement that `nearby_entities()` and `get_entity()` are ground-truth escape hatches, and that using them to fly a fighter means the AI is reading the server's memory, because it is. `docs/sandbox.md` gains the operator's version: if a spawned AI seems to be ignoring you, check whether it can *see* you — that is now a real question with a real answer rather than a bug report. **The documented Lua example is now an executed test** (the acceptance bullet is "examples run against the implemented Lua API", and an example that has never been run is a promise, not a fact) — **which immediately found a real bug**: the Lua `state` table had no `faction` field, so the documented `c.faction ~= state.faction` IFF check compared against `nil`, every contact looked hostile, and the guide's own AI would have opened fire on its wingman. `state.faction` is now exposed and a friendly-fire test pins it
- **engine**: Weather and night degrade detection (#209) — the curves the `SensingEnvironment` seam was landed for. `environmentPodScale(type, env)` scales probability of detection per **channel**, and the four channels are deliberately **not alike**, because that asymmetry is the entire reason an aircraft carries more than one kind of sensor: in a full storm the eyeball keeps **11%** of its chance, IR 36%, and radar **78%** (a little rain clutter — that indifference to weather is most of why radar exists); at night, **visual drops to 25% and nothing else changes at all** — a jet engine is exactly as hot at midnight, radar does not care what time it is, so **the dark is the eyeball's problem and nobody else's**, which is why night attacks work and why an IR-equipped aircraft owns the night. Two properties are load-bearing and both are pinned by tests. **Clear daylight costs exactly nothing — 1.0, not 0.99** — because every authored `pod` is quoted against fair weather, so anything else would make every content pack's numbers quietly mean something slightly different than they say (and would shift every baseline in #684). And **no condition ever drives a channel to zero**: the worst weather leaves a small floor, so a target is never *mathematically* undetectable — acquiring it just takes a very long time. A hard zero would be an invisibility cloak issued by the weather. The weights are behavioural numbers, not measured physics, chosen so the channels stay meaningfully different; they are documented as such, with the full table in `docs/modding/formats.md` so an author can see what their `pod` is actually worth in the conditions their mission ships with
- **ai**: Sensing-gated conditions, honest targeting, and the template migration (#690) — **the user-visible heart of #670**: the AI now acts on what it has DETECTED, not on what is there. New `StateMachineController` conditions reading `AiTickContext::contacts` (`DetectedHostileWithinRange`, `DetectsThreatWithinRange`, `LostContact`, `HasLockedContact`, `NoContacts`), and a new `engine/ai/TargetView.h` through which **every** targeting controller (pursuit, evade, break-turn, lead/lag, both yo-yos) now resolves its target — that single choke point is the point: a controller **cannot** reach past the sensors into the `EntityManager` any more, because there is exactly one place that answers "where is my target" and it goes through the contact table. Three behaviours fall straight out of the model and are each pinned by a test: a hostile the entity **has not detected does not trigger anything** (the #670 acceptance criterion — the bandit is alive, hostile and 1 km away, and as far as the AI is concerned it is not there); a detected contact **does not trigger before the reaction delay** has elapsed (`Contact::reacted` — seeing is not noticing, and an AI that ignored this would have superhuman reflexes on every difficulty); and a **coasting** contact still counts, at its **last-known** position, so a target that beams or masks does not make the AI twitch back to patrol — it keeps flying at the memory until the coast expires, which is exactly what a coast is for. The `patrol_attack` and `escort` factory templates migrate to the honest triggers with **no operator-visible change**: `spawn --ai patrol_attack <idx>` means what it always did, and now means it honestly. **Null contacts stay normative** — when sensing was not evaluated (unit tests, headless callers) every new condition falls back to its ground-truth ancestor, which is why the whole suite passes with **zero** behavioural diffs on the old paths and why the migration was safe to do in one step. `HasLockedContact` and `NoContacts` deliberately return **false** with no sensing, rather than falling back: an entity that never looked must not be able to claim a lock it never took, nor conclude "all clear". `NoContacts` has **no grace timer**, also deliberately — the sensor's own authored `lock_hold_s` coast already provides the hysteresis, and a second invented timer in the condition would be a worse copy of a mechanism the content already owns
- **ai**: Lua `detected_contacts()` — the honest view for scripted AI (#691). Lua controllers could only "see" through `nearby_entities()`, a raw radius query over ground truth: it looks through terrain, through the back of the aircraft's head, and to any range asked for. Scripts now get the same contact table the C++ AI does — only what the entity has actually **detected**, with **last-known** position and velocity, so a *coasting* contact reports where the target **was**. Each entry carries `idx`, `state` (`detected`/`locked`/`coasting`), `pos`/`vel` (last-known), **`age_s`** (seconds since the target was last really seen — 0 while visible, growing while coasting, which is precisely when a script should stop trusting `pos`), `reacted`, `faction`, and **`sensor_types`** (which kinds hold it). That last one needed a new `Contact::sensorTypeMask`: "he has me on radar" and "he can see me" are different tactical facts, and RWR/EMCON (#526/#529) will need exactly this distinction — so it is recorded rather than collapsed. Two fields from the issue's sketch are deliberately **absent**: `quality`, because the contact model has no quality signal and inventing a plausible number would be lying to scripts about how much they know; and a `tracking` state, because the model has `Detected`/`Locked`/`Coasting` and a fourth name for one of them would be a fiction. `detected_contacts()` returns `{}` when sensing was not evaluated (headless/tests), so every existing script keeps working. **`get_entity(idx)` still returns live ground truth and is deliberately NOT restricted** — existing scripts depend on it — but the modding docs now say plainly that a script reading `get_entity` on a coasting contact is quietly cheating, and that `nearby_entities()` is an omniscient query; both carry pointers to the honest API. `docs/modding/ai.md` documents the new API with a worked example, including why `reacted` is not a formality (a script that ignores it has an AI with superhuman reflexes on every difficulty setting)
- **engine**: Server-side difficulty — the `[ai] difficulty` preset reaches the sim tick (#682). Until now `AiScaling` (`radarSensorRange`, `reactionTimeS`, …) was parsed and round-tripped **client-side only**, and fl-server knew nothing beyond an `[ai] difficulty_floor` string it never acted on — so the honest-sensing pass had two inputs it could not get and #685 deliberately left `ctx.difficulty` **null** rather than defaulting it (which would have silently applied the *Cadet* preset and halved every radar range on a server that never chose a difficulty). This closes that gap: `[ai] difficulty` (`cadet|pilot|ace`, default `pilot`) is resolved at startup to an `AiScaling` and injected via `WorldBroadcaster::setAiScaling`, reaching controllers through `AiTickContext::difficulty` and the sensing pass through `radarSensorRange` (a fraction on **radar** max-range) and `reactionTimeS` (the base acquisition→action delay, scaled per-unit by `EntityDef [ai].reaction`). **It gates ACTING, not SEEING** — a Cadet AI detects a target at the same instant an Ace one does, it just takes longer to do anything about it (and, on radar, has to be closer to detect it at all). A knob that made the low-difficulty AI's *eyes* worse would be a different and dishonest thing, and the split is why `reactionTimeS` and `radarSensorRange` are separate fields rather than one "skill" scalar. The preset table is **mod-overridable** (`data/difficulty.toml` through the AssetManager, highest-priority pack wins — the same path the client already uses), so a pack tunes its own AI without patching the server. Hot-reloadable via `reload_config`, which resolves the preset **off the sim thread** (it may read a pack asset) and passes only the resulting POD across the callback; a null resolver leaves the running scaling untouched rather than resetting it to a default. `reload_config` also now actually applies `[world] sensor_check_hz`, which #685 documented as hot-reloadable and did not wire — the doc was ahead of the code. `[ai] difficulty` and `[ai] difficulty_floor` are documented as the distinct things they are: the former is what the server RUNS, the latter is the future per-client clamp
- **netcode**: The sensing pass — `SensorSystem` and `TickPhase::Sensing` in the parallel tick (#685). This is the task that makes honest sensing **true at runtime** rather than merely computable: every sensor-equipped entity now gets a per-tick **contact table** built from cones and probability, and `AiTickContext::contacts` is what a controller reads. **Ground truth is no longer reachable from a consumer** — an omniscient AI cannot be written by accident, because the ground-truth position is not on the path (2026-07-12 decision record). New `engine/sensor/SensorSystem.{h,cpp}` owning per-observer side-storage (the `m_controlledEntities` idiom — `EntityState` stays a flat POD that knows nothing about being observed), a new `TickPhase::Sensing` evaluated after the spatial rebuild and **before** the AI pass (so a controller steers on contacts detected from the same pre-step world), and `WorldBroadcaster::setSensorDefResolver` in the `setFlightModelResolver` pattern (engine-net must not link engine-content). **PLAYERS ARE OBSERVERS TOO** — player avionics (#526) will inherit exactly these semantics rather than growing a parallel "what can the player see" path, which is the entire point of one vocabulary with three consumers. **Serial-equivalent by construction, which is a correctness requirement and not a nicety:** an observer reads only const world state and writes only its own slot, and every die is seeded from `(observer, target, tick, slot, lobe)` rather than drawn from shared RNG state — so the contact tables are byte-identical on 1 worker and on 16, and the same server cannot make different decisions depending on how many cores it happens to have (covered by a reverse-order equivalence test and a clean TSan run). Checks are **staggered** across the cadence window (`(tick + entityIdx) % stride`) so the world's sensing cost spreads evenly instead of one tick in six carrying all of it. `[world] sensor_check_hz` (default **10** — the reference cadence every authored `pod` is tuned against; `[1, 60]`, hot-reloadable) is documented as **re-tuning every content pack on the server**, not as a CPU dial. Four calls worth stating: an entity with **no declared sensors gets the builtin eyeball** (honest sensing is the default everywhere, including the zero-content sandbox — no pack can opt out by omission); a **coasting contact reports where the target WAS**, never a fresh position it has not earned; the **reaction delay gates `Contact::reacted`, not detection** (a rookie *sees* you exactly when an ace does — what a rookie does is take longer to act, and a difficulty knob that made him see less would be a lie); and the contact cap (32/observer) truncates by **relevance, not entity index**, because dropping the enemy on your nose in favour of a friendly with a lower index is the kind of bug that only shows up in a dogfight. Difficulty scaling is left **unset** (`ctx.difficulty == nullptr`, radar at its authored range, no reaction delay) rather than defaulted to `AiScaling{}`, whose values are the *Cadet preset* — silently halving every radar range on a server that never configured a difficulty would be a lie; #682 wires it. `engine-sensor` picks up its `engine-spatial` PUBLIC edge (the last one the decision record specified) and `engine-net` PUBLIC-links it
- **engine**: Dual-lobe detection math with deterministic probability rolls (#684) — `engine/sensor/Detection.{h,cpp}`: cone containment, signature-scaled effective range, a deterministic per-check acquisition roll, and the search→track→coast contact state machine. Pure functions, no tick machinery and no storage — `SensorSystem` (#685) owns all of that and calls in here. **The central design call is that PoD gates ACQUISITION and geometry MAINTAINS the contact.** A probability of detection is the chance of *finding* something you were not already looking at; it is not re-rolled to keep a contact you already hold. Re-rolling for retention would make a 0.35-PoD radar drop and re-acquire the same untouched target several times a second — a flicker that is neither physical nor playable and that no downstream smoothing can honestly repair — so a target already inside the cone stays held without a die, and is lost when it leaves the cone (or when its coast expires), never when a die comes up short. Rolls are therefore owed only on the Lost→Detected and Detected→Locked edges. **Determinism is a correctness requirement, not a nicety:** the acquisition die is seeded from `(observer, target, tick, sensorSlot, lobe)` with the same construction as the per-entity turbulence RNG in `WorldBroadcaster::stepFlightSim` — no shared RNG state is mutated, so the result depends on *who* and *when* and never on evaluation order, and a sensing pass cannot hand a 4-worker server different contacts than a 1-worker one. The threshold comparison is **integer** (a 24-bit domain, high bits of the hash) precisely because a float compare would put the platform-independence guarantee at the mercy of compiler float ordering. `Coasting` is a first-class state: a lock that loses its target runs out `lock_hold_s` on last-known state before dropping, and re-entering the cone during the coast recovers it **without a new roll** — it was never lost, only unobserved. A search-only sensor (the eyeball, `lock_hold_s = 0`) drops the instant the target leaves its cone, which is the honest reading: you have not "lost track" of something you were only ever looking at. Losing the *track* lobe while still in the *search* lobe demotes to `Detected` rather than coasting — we can still see it, we just cannot shoot it. Radar range scales by `sqrt(rcs)` and the other channels linearly (decision record), and the difficulty knob (`AiScaling::radarSensorRange`) applies to radar only; skill maps affinely so the **default `[ai] skill` of 0.5 is exactly unity** — an entity that authors no `[ai]` section detects at precisely the probability its sensor def states. The `emitting` flag gates radar/laser **track** lobes only (a passive IRST does not need to radiate to stare at you). `SensingEnvironment` is landed as an identity seam so #209's night/weather curves have somewhere to attach without re-cutting every signature here. `engine-sensor` now PUBLIC-links `engine-entity` (for `SignatureDef`, picking up GLM through its `platform-hal` edge) — note the direction: **the observed does not depend on the observer**, which is why the signature POD lives in `engine/entity/`. One defect found and fixed by its own test: the coast was run down by repeated float subtraction, whose accumulated dust (~1.5e-6 s) left an expired coast marginally above zero and held a contact one extra check — 100 ms past its `lock_hold_s`, every time, at the 10 Hz reference cadence
- **entity**: Signatures, sensor suite and per-unit AI tuning on entity definitions (#680) — the **target** side of detection, where #679's `SensorDef` is the observer side. New `engine/entity/SignatureDef.h` (`SignatureDef` + `AiTuning`, dependency-free PODs next to `DamageDef.h`) and three optional TOML sections on `parseEntityDef`: `[signatures]` (`rcs`/`ir`/`visual`/`laser`), an `entity.sensors` id list, and `[ai]` (`skill`/`reaction`). **`engine-entity` acquires no new link dependency, which is the point**: the entity layer describes what an entity *looks like* and names the sensors it carries as plain strings, so the thing being observed does not depend on the thing observing it (the decision record's rule, enforced by `cmake/layering.cmake`). **Signatures are unitless multipliers against a baseline fighter (1.0)** — the value a sensor def's ranges are already quoted against — with radar scaling by `sqrt(rcs)` and IR/visual linearly; the four channels are **independent**, so a stealth airframe with a tiny `rcs` stays exactly as hot to an IRST unless the author says otherwise. **Zero is rejected along with the negatives**: a signature of `0` is not "very stealthy", it is a target that sensor type can *never* detect at any range, and an author who wants that should say so with a number rather than get it by typo. **Defaults are load-bearing, not filler** — an entity that declares nothing is exactly as detectable as every sensor def already assumes, so existing packs keep working and the sensing pass has no special case for "unauthored". **An empty `sensors` list means the same as no list: the builtin eyeball**, never blindness and never omniscience — a pack cannot opt out of honest sensing by omission. An **unknown sensor id is deliberately not a parse error**: a pack's cross-references resolve only once every file in it has been read, so that check belongs at resolve time as a warning, and one missing sensor should not stop an aircraft from loading with the rest of its suite. What the parser *does* reject is a list that cannot mean anything (a non-array, an empty id, a duplicate). `skill` and `reaction` are two knobs rather than one difficulty scalar because they are genuinely different properties: a veteran spots a contact early **and** acts on it immediately, while a distracted SAM crew can see you perfectly well and still be slow off the mark. Their consumers land with the sensing pass (#685) and the reaction-delay task (#690)
- **content**: Sensor definitions as a first-class content-pack asset type (#679) — the schema half of the sensor core locked by the 2026-07-12 decision record. New `engine-sensor` library (`fl::sensor`): `SensorDef` (dual **search**/**track** lobes — az/el half-angles, min/max range, per-check probability of detection — plus `omnidirectional` and `emitter`), `parseSensorDef`, and a compiled-in `BuiltinSensors::eyeball()`. **The point of the type is that there is only one of it.** Player avionics (#526), AI detection (#670) and missile seekers (#628/#676) were each about to grow their own idea of "what can this thing see", and the repo already carried two vocabularies for the same physical question (`[seeker] fov_deg/acquisition_nm` on weapons, `[radar] track_range_nm/can_shutdown` on ground units); all three now read the same def. A radar, an IRST, a laser designator and a human eyeball differ in their *parameters*, not in their model. `sensors/*.toml` loads through the normal priority stack, so a theater pack can re-tune an aircraft's radar **without forking the aircraft** — the whole reason this is an asset type rather than a hard-coded table. **The builtin eyeball is what makes honest sensing the default rather than an opt-in feature flag**: an AI entity that declares no sensors gets it, in every configuration of the engine including the zero-content-pack sandbox, so there is no setup in which an AI sees through terrain and no pack can produce one by omission. It is deliberately search-only — an eyeball *finds* an aircraft, it does not hold a lock on one, and that asymmetry is the model, not a missing field. Two schema calls depart from the issue text and are worth stating: the def nests under a **`[sensor]` root table** (matching `[weapon]`/`[entity]`, rather than bare top-level keys), and ranges are **authored in nautical miles and stored in SI** (matching the weapon parser) — the `[radar]` fragment this schema replaces already says `track_range_nm`, so migrating a pack is a rename rather than a unit conversion, and an author does not quote radar range in metres and missile range in miles in the same pack. `emitter` is landed as a **seam, not a feature**: radar and laser *track* lobes will require it, and it is what RWR, EMCON and SAM radar shutdown hang off (#526/#529) without those having to retrofit the sensor core. **PoD is per check at the reference 10 Hz cadence**, documented as such — a probability without a rate is meaningless, and it is not silently renormalized when an operator changes the cadence. The parser **throws** rather than defaulting, like its `parseFlightModel`/`parseWeaponDef` siblings: a sensor that quietly fell back to defaults is an aircraft whose radar became an eyeball, which is worse than a load error. New fuzz harness (`fuzz_sensor_def_toml`) + dictionary + seed corpus, matching the other TOML parsers. `engine-sensor` stays dep-free for now (schema + parser + builtin need to know nothing about entities); the decision record's PUBLIC edges on `engine-entity`/`engine-spatial` arrive with the detection math (#684), which is the first code here that actually observes anything
- **tools**: `validate-sensor` CLI (#679). Joins the `validate-flight-model`/`validate-weapon`/`validate-mission` family and follows #624's rule — it **delegates schema validity to the engine's own parser** and only reports what that throws, so validator and runtime cannot drift and a sensor this tool passes is a sensor the engine loads. On top of the parser it adds what a parser deliberately must not do: **plausibility warnings** for defs that are schema-perfect and still nonsense — a passive (visual/IR) sensor with `emitter = true` (which would light up an RWR that should stay quiet), a **non-emitting radar carrying a `[track]` lobe it can therefore never use**, a track lobe that reaches further / sees wider / detects more reliably than the search lobe that must feed it (a sensor that can hold what it can never find), a track with `lock_hold_s = 0` (locks that flicker on a single failed check), and ranges implausible enough to be the classic metres-typed-into-a-miles-field slip. None fail the build — content packs are allowed to be strange on purpose. Wired into the existing tool smoke steps (Linux/macOS + Windows) and `.vscode/tasks.json`
- **ai**: Scripted wingman command grammar, the formation/command hierarchy it is issued against, and the in-flight radio menu (#610). This is the **zero-AI half of Epic O** and the CI-tested path everything else degrades to: with no LLM provider — or on a CPU-only server, which cannot meet the 2 s radio-comms budget (#769) — this *is* the wingman. It closes the Phase 4 acceptance criterion ("wingman follows player and responds to all six commands"), and nothing about it existed before: there was no wingman, no formation, no way for an AI to follow a **moving** player, and no client→server gameplay message at all. **The unit of organisation is the FORMATION, not "the wingman."** Real air operations are tiered — an element sits inside a flight sits inside a package, with a mission commander above and AWACS/GCI directing from outside the chain — so a formation is one node of a command tree (`engine/world/Formation.h`: `{id, parent, callsign, anchor, commander, members, children}`) and every case is a *shape* of it rather than a special case bolted on: a player with one AI wingman is one node with one AI member; two humans flying as a pair is one node with one **human** member; an all-AI flight under an AWACS is a node whose commander **is not in it**; a strike package is a parent node with child flights. Two properties are load-bearing. **The commander is a role, not a seat** — authority is "do you command this node *or an ancestor of it*", which cascades down the tree, so a package commander can order a flight inside the package without being that flight's own commander; and it is checked against the *sender*, never against anything in the packet (an order naming another peer's flight returns the **same** code as an unknown flight, so the channel cannot be used to enumerate who leads what). **A member is an aircraft, not an AI** — AI members get their controller retasked, while a **human** member cannot be retasked at all, so the order is **relayed to them as a radio call** (`WingmanResult::Relayed`) and compliance is their choice. Pretending a person can be commanded like an autopilot would have been the dishonest design, and the distinction is visible on the wire. **`FormationController` is the genuinely new capability**: it holds station on a **moving** lead. The existing `escort` template captures its orbit centre once at construction — it escorts *the place the lead was standing when the order was given*, and its own comment says the controller that tracks a moving lead is missing. Slots are **indexed** rather than named, so a flight of 2 and a flight of 8 use the same geometry, and throttle is **closed-loop** on along-track error and closure rate — a fixed throttle overshoots a slow lead and never catches a fast one, which is the whole difference between chasing and keeping station. **`attack_my_target` resolves by boresight, not by a lock**, because there is no sensor or targeting system yet (#677/#684/#526): the server already receives the player's look direction at 60 Hz in `MsgClientInput::viewAxis`, so designation uses state the server *owns* rather than anything newly trusted from the client, and needs no wire change. Nothing in the cone **refuses** the order (`NoTarget`, "Two, no joy") rather than substituting a target — an attack order that quietly picks its own target is worse than one that declines, which is the same principle the eval suite's out-of-grammar cases are built on. It sits behind a `TargetDesignator` std::function, so a real radar lock re-points it with **one lambda swap**: no grammar change, no wire change, no client change. The six commands are **flat and parameterless** on purpose (`engine/ai/WingmanCommand.h`, the single source of truth): intent latency on CPU is *prompt-eval dominated*, so the vocabulary IS the #769 latency lever — a flat enum is the shortest possible prompt, makes constrained decoding a one-line alternation, and lets the eval harness score by string equality with **no code change**. `unknown` is the intent mapper's *decline* sentinel and is deliberately **not** an executable command, so a model that correctly refuses an out-of-grammar utterance cannot accidentally order the wingman. Two additive MsgIds (`0x0D` order, `0x0E` ack); `kProtocolVersion` stays **1**. The ack carries a result **code**, never server-authored text — brevity calls are UI strings that must be localizable (the `MsgConnectRefusal` precedent), the client needs the same table for its menu labels anyway, and it keeps a server-controlled string off the HUD text path. **The radio menu (`C`) is non-modal**, which is the load-bearing UX call: `GameConsole` suppresses flight input while open, which leaves `MsgClientInput` at its defaults — i.e. *opening the console sends throttle = 0*. Survivable for a debug console; inheriting it for a combat radio menu would idle the engine mid-dogfight. The aircraft keeps flying and only the discrete keys the menu consumes are gated. `engine-net` does **not** link `engine-ai` and must not: the three places an order needs to build a controller are `std::function` hooks that `fl-server` fills in (the `setFlightModelResolver` precedent), which also keeps `test_world_broadcaster` free of `engine-ai` — it is in the TSan target set. The **`flight` admin command family** (`list`/`create`/`add`/`order`/`disband`) is the game-master and AWACS surface: it builds and orders formations it is not part of, including all-AI flights and nested packages, and it **bypasses the peer-authority check visibly** rather than forging a peer identity. Both paths dispatch through one `dispatchOrder`, so a console order and a radio order cannot behave differently — which is also what makes it the future MCP-allowlistable surface. The GM has no boresight, so `flight order <id> attack_my_target` **refuses with an explanation** instead of silently degrading to "hold station"

### Changed

- **ai**: `attack_my_target` now designates from the lead's **contact table**, closing the seam #610 cut. When the scripted wingman shipped there was no sensor system, so "my target" was resolved by **boresight over ground truth** — the hostile nearest the lead's look axis, whether or not the lead could actually see it — and the code said, in as many words, that when the sensor framework landed this would become one lambda swap and nothing else would change. It did, and nothing else did: not the grammar, not the wire, not the client, not the radio menu. New `fl::ai::designateFromContacts` picks from what the lead has **detected**, preferring what it has **LOCKED** (a bearing is not a lock) and falling back to the detected contact nearest the nose. **A lead can no longer order an attack on something it has not seen** — pointing at empty sky returns an invalid id, and the order is REFUSED ("Two, no joy") rather than quietly retargeted, which is the principle the whole grammar was built on. A **coasting** contact stays designatable, deliberately: "the guy who just went into the cloud" is a sensible thing to point at, and the wingman inherits the same last-known state the lead has — both are wrong in exactly the same way, which is what being wingmen means. The old boresight path survives only as the fallback for a server with no sensing evaluated, so a degenerate setup still designates rather than refusing every order
- **tools**: The sensing phase reaches the metrics + scale gate, and `kServerTickSchemaVersion` is **frozen at 6** for the rest of primary development (#686). - **tests**: `test_loop` now runs serially under ctest (`RUN_SERIAL`) — it contains **wall-clock rate** assertions (sleep 1 s, expect the loop to have fired ~60 times), and since #787 made the suite parallel by default those ran while ~20 other test binaries saturated the CPU. They were no longer measuring the game loop; they were measuring how busy the runner was, and they flaked on shared macOS CI. Serialising them restores what the assertion is actually for, and costs nothing measurable (the suite still completes in ~18 s)
- **tests**: The ctest suite is now **parallel-safe**, and runs in parallel by default (#787) — `ctest --preset debug` drops from **79 s to 18 s** on a 24-core box (`execution.jobs = 8` on the `debug`/`release`/`*-msvc` test presets, 4 on `asan`; `.vscode/tasks.json` and CI inherit it automatically, since both invoke ctest through `--preset`). The bound is explicit on purpose: CTest reads `jobs = 0` as *unlimited*, not "one per core", and oversubscribes badly enough (53 concurrent processes on a 24-core box) to exhaust the macOS runner's process limit and leave ~1600 tests unable to start. The root cause was one wrong assumption repeated in three places: **a test may not assume it is the only process on the machine.** `catch_discover_tests` registers each `TEST_CASE` as its own ctest test, so under `-j` the cases of a single binary run as **concurrent processes** — and anything a test names by a fixed string is therefore shared with a sibling running right now. (1) **Ports:** `test_network` handed out ports from "reserved ranges", and two cases had even been given *the same* port (19009) — harmless while Catch2 ran them sequentially, a collision the moment they did not. Ports are now **ephemeral**: bind port 0, let the OS choose, and read it back through the new `ENetNetwork::boundPort()`. That is race-free by construction rather than by convention, and a new test cannot reintroduce the bug by copying a port number. (2) **Cross-talk:** every `test_lan_discovery` case bound the *same* discovery port (19200). LAN discovery is a **broadcast** protocol, so a listener on port P receives whatever is sent to port P — including a beacon fired by a sibling test's process. The failures were never bind errors; they were one test seeing another test's traffic ("second immediate tick does not resend" would report a server it never sent). Each case now takes its own OS-assigned port. (3) **Temp paths:** `TempDir` in `test_validate_licenses` built its path from a **per-process static counter**, which is not unique at all — the counter restarts at 1 in every process, so every case claimed `/tmp/fl_lic_test_1` and the first to finish deleted it out from under the others. This one only surfaced under a *parallel ASan* run. New `tests/temp_path.h` (`uniqueTempPath`/`TempDirGuard`) salts the name with a per-process token. Fixed sleeps in the discovery tests are replaced by bounded polling — a `sleep_for(10ms)` that is too short on a loaded machine passes a negative assertion for the wrong reason (the packet had not arrived yet, not that it was rejected). Three `WorldBroadcaster` tests also had to stop depending on **how busy the host is**: the graceful tick-overrun governor (#514) reads *wall-clock* tick time and sheds snapshots when a tick blows its 16.6 ms budget — exactly right in production, and exactly wrong in a test that asserts "a snapshot was sent on tick N", because a loaded machine makes an instrumented tick slow, the governor correctly concludes the server is overloaded, and the snapshot the test waits for is legitimately never sent (it then read `.back()` off an empty vector, which UBSan caught). Those tests now disable the governor, as one existing serial-equivalence test already did for the same reason. Verified with **5 consecutive green parallel runs**, a full parallel ASan run, and by re-running the three under a fully saturated CPU. `coverage` stays **serial** on purpose (concurrent `.gcda` writes corrupt counters), and `tsan` is not run through ctest at all — `ctest --preset tsan` fails ~26 network tests **and always has**, because TSan's instrumentation slows the ENet/GNS handshake past those tests' fixed pump budgets; `tsan.yml` runs its two scoped binaries directly. `docs/development.md` gains a section on what ASan and TSan each actually catch (they are mutually exclusive, and neither subsumes the other), why TSan is scoped to the data-parallel sim, and the three rules a test must follow to stay parallel-safe
